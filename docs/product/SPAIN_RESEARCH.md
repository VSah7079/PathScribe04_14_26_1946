## 1. Cervical cancer screening

### 1.1 National common-portfolio recommendation (verified, Ministerio de Sanidad)

- Target population is women 25 to 65. Since 2019 it is an organised population programme, replacing about 50 years of opportunistic cytology. Source: https://www.sanidad.gob.es/areas/promocionPrevencion/cribado/cribadoCancer/cancerCervix/infoGeneral.htm
- Ages 25 to 29, not adequately vaccinated: cytology every 3 years. Adequately vaccinated: cytology every 3 years, or start at 30, depending on regional implementation. Same source.
- Ages 30 to 65, regardless of vaccination: high-risk HPV test as primary test. Negative: repeat HPV in 5 years. Positive: triage with cytology; if high risk is ruled out, repeat HPV in 1 year. Same source.
- "Adequate" vaccination protection is assessed by the Ponencia de Programa y Registro de Vacunaciones (no schedule is given on the page). Same source.
- Women at high individual risk: individual risk assessment, then specific protocols. Same source.
- Implementation timeline stated: 5 years from 2019 to start the change, 5 more years to approach 100% coverage. Same source.
- Legal basis stated on that page: Orden SCB/480/2019 (BOE-A-2019-6277, page dates it 26 April 2019) added the programme to the SNS common portfolio and introduced HPV testing; Orden SND/454/2025 (9 May 2025, BOE-A-2025-9277, published 13 May 2025) set the current age-based criteria. Page last updated 30 May 2025. Same source.
- Orden SND/454/2025 modifies Annexes I, II, III and VI of Real Decreto 1030/2006 (common portfolio) and its preamble names the modification of the cervical screening programme; it also has a supplementary provision on implementation. The text of the specific changes was truncated in what I could read. Source: https://vlex.es/vid/orden-snd-454-2025-1079715805 (secondary host of the BOE text).
- Older ministry press releases describe the 2019 design as cytology every 3 years at 25 to 34 and HPV at 35 to 65 with a 5-year repeat. These are superseded by the 2025 page above (30 to 65 HPV). Sources: https://www.sanidad.gob.es/gabinete/notasPrensa.do?id=4572 and https://www.sanidad.gob.es/en/gabinete/notasPrensa.do?id=4612. The second states the orders took effect 1 July 2019 and regions had six months to update catalogues; that date conflicts with the 26 April 2019 on the infoGeneral page and I did not resolve it.
- COULD NOT VERIFY: the BOE text of Orden SCB/480/2019 and Orden SND/454/2025 annex wording (BOE fetch blocked). COULD NOT VERIFY: the content of the national "Guía de cribado del cáncer de cuello de útero en España 2025" (a copy hosted by Castilla y León exists but the fetch failed on URL length): https://www.saludcastillayleon.es/profesionales/en/informacion-epidemiologica/registro-poblacional-cancer/programas-prevencion-deteccion-cancer/programa-prevencion-deteccion-precoz-cancer-cuello-utero-ca.files/3022569-Gu%C3%ADa%20de%20cribado%20del%20c%C3%A1ncer%20de%20cuello%20de%20%C3%BAtero%20en%20Espa%C3%B1a%202025.pdf

### 1.2 How autonomous communities (CCAA) run it

- National survey of 15 CCAA (13 communities and 2 autonomous cities), data collected Oct 2023 to Mar 2024, approved by the Ponencia de Cribado Poblacional 26/11/2024 and the Comisión de Salud Pública 23/01/2025. Results are anonymised, so no per-community breakdown is published. Source: https://vsf-iwsold-pre-portal.sanidad.gob.es/areas/promocionPrevencion/cribado/cribadoCancer/cancerCervix/docs/estadoSituacion_CribadoCervixEspana.pdf
  - Programme status: opportunistic only 4; transitioning to population-based 6; transitioning to risk-based population 2; population-based only 1; risk-based population only 1; both 1.
  - Primary test offered: cytology only 4; HPV only 2; both 9.
  - Cytology ages: 25 to 34 in 10 CCAA; 25 to 29 in 2; 25 to 65 in 3. Interval every 3 years if negative in all. Liquid-based cytology in 13 CCAA.
  - HPV ages: 35 to 65 in 7; 35 to 64 in 2; 30 to 65 in 2; HPV not used in 4. Interval every 5 years if negative in all that use HPV.
  - HPV test type: partial genotyping 10; HPV-AR 2; total genotyping 2; mRNA 1 (some report more than one).
  - Triage after abnormal cytology: partial genotyping 9; HPV-AR 3; both 1; total genotyping 1; unspecified genotyping 1.
  - Invited population: 25 to 65 in 14 CCAA; 35 to 65 in 1.
  - Implication: a first release cannot hard-code one age/interval rule; it must be configurable per community and per programme.
- Castilla y León (official regional page): 25 to 64 inclusive (page also says general criteria list 25 to 65); 25 to 29 cytology every 3 years (vaccinated women: either cytology every 3 years or start at 30, depending on implementation); 30 to 65 HPV-AR; negative repeat at 5 years; positive triage, repeat HPV at 1 year if high risk ruled out. It refers to a 2022 AEPCC guide for abnormal-result management. The page does not describe Bethesda, result communication or registry link. Source: https://www.saludcastillayleon.es/profesionales/es/programas-guias-clinicas/programas-salud/programa-prevencion-deteccion-precoz-cancer-cuello-utero-ca
- Catalonia: conference abstract (Gaceta Sanitaria 2025) on an HPV self-sampling pilot (July 2021 to Dec 2023, 6,355 women invited, pharmacy kit distribution, SMS invitations, triage cytology for HPV-positive women). States a population-programme pilot phase from 2024 and planned implementation 2025 to 2029. No ages or intervals given. Source: https://gacetasanitaria.org/es-download-pdf-S0213911125005837
- Andalucía: official page was navigation only; ages and tests not extracted. A hospital teaching presentation (not primary) reports Andalucía as cytology 25 to 34 every 3 years, HPV 35 to 65 every 5 years, staggered invitation 2024 to 2029, results sent by letter, and "no concluyente" repeat in 3 months. Treat as unverified: https://www.huvn.es/archivos/cms/ginecologia-y-obstetricia/archivos/publico/clases_residentes/2025/CRIBADO%20POBLACIONAL%20CA%CC%81NCER%20DE%20CE%CC%81RVIX%20ESPAN%CC%83A.%20Clase%202025.%20(1)%20(4).pdf
- COULD NOT VERIFY: ages/intervals for Madrid, Valencia, Basque Country, Galicia, Navarra and other individual communities. COULD NOT VERIFY: whether self-sampling is nationally recommended (only the Catalan pilot found).

### 1.3 Result wording / classification

- Spanish consensus screening guidelines (October 2014; SEAP, SEC, SEGO, AEPCC) are cited by a Castilla-La Mancha hospital pathology presentation that uses Bethesda-style categories: Normal; squamous ASCUS, ASC-H, LSIL (CIN 1), HSIL (CIN 2-3), squamous carcinoma; glandular AGC (endocervical/endometrial, not otherwise specified), AGC favour neoplasia, endocervical adenocarcinoma in situ, adenocarcinoma. It also quotes "El sistema Bethesda ha permitido una nomenclatura reproducible y entendible" and SEC quality indicators (ASC/SIL ratio below 3). This is a hospital presentation (author at a SESCAM hospital), not the guideline itself. This is the 2011 White Paper era text (it cites LO 15/1999). Source: https://seap.es/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Reglas-y-Consejos-Buenas-Practicas-Profesionales.pdf

COULD NOT VERIFY: whether there is a Spanish-language official translation of Bethesda 2014 terms. The national status report, the Castilla y León page and the HPV-teaching presentation I read do not name Bethesda. ## 2. Cancer registries

### 2.1 National level

- Real Decreto 568/2024, 18 June 2024 (BOE-A-2024-12379, BOE 148, 19 June 2024; in force 20 June 2024) created the Red Estatal de Vigilancia en Salud Pública. Article 13.2.a says non-communicable disease surveillance systems "incluirán el Sistema de Vigilancia de Cáncer", but the decree does not set out its scope; art. 13.3 requires a separate royal decree within three years (second final provision). Art. 12.1 obliges public and private entities and persons to give data to health authorities; systems must comply with ENS and ENI (art. 19.1). The decree does not mention anatomic pathology data or population registries by name. Source: https://www.boe.es/eli/es/rd/2024/06/18/568
- Status: as of April 2026 the specific regulatory decree for the Cancer Surveillance System and a planned National Cancer Registry was still pending; design draws on ENCR and IARC methods. Source: https://eurohealthobservatory.who.int/monitors/pace/case-studies/pace/pace-spain-2025/cancer-surveillance-system-within-the-state-public-health-surveillance-network-spain (European Observatory case study; secondary but institutional).
- REDECAN is the cooperative network of population cancer registries, created November 2010. Cases defined with ICD-O-3 topography and morphology. Source: https://www.ciberisciii.es/media/914374/dossier-redecan-20072017.pdf (dossier lists member registries: Albacete, Asturias, Canarias, Comunidad Valenciana, Cuenca, Ciudad Real, Girona, Granada, La Rioja, Mallorca, Murcia, Navarra, País Vasco, Tarragona, plus RETI-SEHOP for childhood tumours; this is a 2017 document, current membership not confirmed).
- A SEE presentation lists 13 IARC-accredited registries covering 27% of the population and states that data sources include anatomic pathology and private anatomic pathology laboratories. Source: https://www.seepidemiologia.es/documents/dummy/Mesa-2-Registros-Poblacionales-2.pdf (presentation, not law; date unknown).

### 2.2 Regional (who a pathology lab reports to, legal basis)

Registries are regional, so the reporting obligation and channel differ by community. Confirmed examples:

- Andalucía: Decreto 586/2019 (29 Oct 2019, BOJA 212, 4 Nov 2019) regulates the Registro de Cáncer de Andalucía, replacing Decreto 297/2007. Public and private health centres and professionals who diagnose, treat or follow cancer must provide the Annex II data (art. 4.2); non-compliance is an infraction under Ley 2/1998 (art. 25); data is sent electronically per procedure and deadline set by the Registry Directorate (art. 5.1), without anonymisation (art. 4.4). Annex I sources include anatomic pathology databases. Legal basis cited includes GDPR art. 9.2.i and f, LOPDGDD DA 17, Ley 33/2011, Ley 16/2011, Ley 41/2002 art. 16.3. Source: https://cdn.juntadeandalucia.es/sites/default/files/2024-07/Decreto%20586-2019.pdf . Data protection record (controller: Dirección General de Salud Pública y Ordenación Farmacéutica; data: name, DNI/NIF, address, NUSHA, birth, sex, health data; no recipients or international transfers planned): https://www.juntadeandalucia.es/protecciondedatos/detalle/166297.html
- Castilla y León: Orden SAN/205/2005, 8 February 2005 created the Registro Poblacional de Cáncer. Any public or private health centre, professional or institution holding cancer case data is a source (art. 5.1) and is required to supply it; provincial units receive electronic or paper notification forms in the format the Observatorio de Salud Pública determines; hospitals name a responsible professional. Processing follows IARC criteria (art. 3). Source: https://www.saludcastillayleon.es/en/enfermedades-problemas-salud/cancer/registro-poblacional-cancer.files/2585189-BOCYL-D-22022005-1.pdf (cites LO 15/1999, now superseded by GDPR/LOPDGDD; whether the order was amended later: COULD NOT VERIFY).
- Illes Balears: Sistema de Vigilancia Epidemiológica del Cáncer started 2024, records only breast, cervix, colon and rectal cancer for now (more to be added), and cites RD 568/2024. The page does not say who notifies or how labs report. Source: https://caib.es/sites/epidemiologia/es/vigilancia_de_cancer/
- Comunidad de Madrid: population registry sits in the Red de Vigilancia Epidemiológica (Decreto 184/1996), is a REDECAN/ENCR member per the page, with a paediatric registry RECAM-i; the page does not describe sources or notification duty. Source: https://www.comunidad.madrid/node/74791
- Cataluña: statistical registry listed under Ley 13/2010 (Plan estadístico); the page names no sources or notification process. Source: https://www.sanidad.gob.es/estadEstudios/estadisticas/estadisticas/Rebeca2017/Catalunya.pdf

### 3.2 B2B e-invoicing and public-sector e-invoicing

- B2B: Ley 18/2022 "Crea y Crece"; Real Decreto 238/2026 (25 March 2026, published 31 March 2026); AEAT order on the public solution (SPFE) published 5 October 2026 (BOE-A-2026-20587 per PwC; the B2BRouter blog names it Orden HAC/1028/2026, a discrepancy I did not resolve). Invoices are mandatory when the recipient is a public administration under art. 2 of Ley 39/2015 (art. 2.2.f). ### 3.4 Tax identifiers

- All natural-person and entity NIFs have 9 characters. DNI: 8 digits plus control letter. NIE: letter (X, Y, Z), 7 digits, control letter. AEAT-assigned NIFs without DNI/NIE: K (Spanish minors under 14 resident), L (Spanish citizens non-resident), M (foreigners without NIE). Entity NIFs: letter for legal form (A, B, C, D, E, F, G, H, J, U, V domestic; N and W foreign; P local corporations; Q public bodies; R religious congregations; S State/regional organs), 7 digits, control character (digit or letter A to J depending on type). Control character algorithm for natural persons: same for all; for NIE, X=0, Y=1, Z=2 prefix substitution. Legal basis listed: Real Decreto 1065/2007, Orden EHA/451/2008, Real Decreto 338/1990. The 2008 AEAT document does not give the letter lookup table. Source: https://www.caib.es/sites/civitasmanualsisuport/f/41519 (hosted AEAT 2008 document; later AEAT changes COULD NOT be checked).
- Patient-side identifiers in the SNS clinical report (RD 1093/2010 Annex V): name, surname, birth date, sex, NASS, regional CIP and clinical history number mandatory; DNI/residence permit/passport, SNS code, European CIP recommended. Source: https://vsf-iwsold-pro-01.mscbs.gob.es/organizacion/sns/planCalidadSNS/docs/REAL_DECRETO_1093-2010_CMDIC.pdf

## 4. Terminology

### 4.1 SNOMED CT (Spanish edition)

- Spain has been a SNOMED International member since 2009; Ministerio de Sanidad (Centro Nacional de Referencia) manages licences. An affiliate licence is required to download and is free "al ser España miembro de SNOMED International". Eligibility: anyone residing in Spain or "whose professional activity is with a national company" through the Área de Descarga; the applicant accepts the SNOMED CT Affiliate License Agreement. Questions: semanticaSNS@sanidad.gob.es. Source: https://www.sanidad.gob.es/areas/saludDigital/interoperabilidadSemantica/factoriaRecursos/snomedCT/preguntas.htm
- Release cadence: International Edition monthly (1st); Spanish Edition quarterly (10 Feb, May, Aug, Nov); Spain SNS extension twice a year (June, December); Spain medicines extension monthly. National extensions depend on specific International Edition versions. Same source.
- Download area products include the Spanish Edition (descriptions only, must be used with the International Edition), the International Edition including ICD-10 and ICD-O-3.1 map refsets, SNS extension, medicines extension. Source: https://www.sanidad.gob.es/eu/areas/saludDigital/interoperabilidadSemantica/docs/GuiaContenidosAreaDescarga_2026.pdf . Files are RF2 text in ZIP, UTF-8 (https://www.sanidad.gob.es/areas/saludDigital/interoperabilidadSemantica/factoriaRecursos/snomedCT/referenciaSNOMED/contenidosSNOMED.htm).

## 5. Data protection and health records

- GDPR and LOPDGDD (Ley Orgánica 3/2018): the BOE text (https://www.boe.es/boe/dias/2018/12/06/pdfs/BOE-A-2018-16673.pdf) in what I could read confirms art. 9 (Spanish law with rank of law may support GDPR 9.2.g/h/i processing, including health data for healthcare services) and art. 7 (minors' consent from 14). The body of Disposición adicional decimoséptima (health/biomedical research data) and whether art. 34 requires a DPO for healthcare entities were NOT in the fetched text: COULD NOT VERIFY. The Andalusian registry decree cites DA 17 and GDPR 9.2.i/f as the basis for the registry (see 2.2).
- ENS (Real Decreto 311/2022): applies to the whole public sector and to private-sector systems of companies that provide services or solutions to public entities under contract (the supplier's security policy must be approved by its top body); public contracts must require ENS conformity declarations/certifications, potentially including the supply chain; categories BÁSICA / MEDIA / ALTA; MEDIA and ALTA need a certification audit, BÁSICA a self-assessment; audits at least every two years; existing systems had 24 months to adapt. Where personal data is processed, GDPR/LOPDGDD risk analysis and DPIA (art. 35) apply and stricter measures prevail. Source: https://boe.es/boe/dias/2022/05/04/pdfs/BOE-A-2022-7191.pdf . Consequence: PathScribe sold to a public Spanish health service is likely to be asked for ENS conformity as a supplier (inference from art. 2 text). COULD NOT VERIFY: the category any specific health service assigns.
- Clinical record retention, state level: Ley 41/2002 art. 17.1: at least five years from the date of discharge of each care process (or longer as needed for care); longer retention for judicial, epidemiological, research or SNS-organisation purposes, with identification avoided where possible; records not necessarily in original format; custody responsibility lies with the centre's management; data protection security rules apply. Source: https://vhir.vallhebron.com/sites/default/files/2021-06/25.%20Ley%2041-2002%20Autonmia%20del%20paciente.pdf (university-hosted copy of the law). Art. 15 lists pathology among minimum record contents (same copy). AEPD report confirms the 5-year minimum and that some regional rules set much longer periods: https://www.aepd.es/documento/2017-0014.pdf
- Regional example: Cataluña, Ley 16/2010 (BOE 156, 28 June 2010) amends Ley 21/2000 art. 12: anatomic pathology reports, necropsy reports, surgical reports, consent forms and complementary exploration reports must be kept at least 15 years from discharge of each care process; other documents 5 years. Source: https://boe.es/boe/dias/2010/06/28/pdfs/BOE-A-2010-10215.pdf . COULD NOT VERIFY: retention rules in other communities (the AEPD note says they exist and can be longer).
- Blocks and slides: no statute fixing a period for blocks and slides was found. SEAP recommendations (Revista Española de Patología, 2024, DOI 10.1016/j.patol.2024.04.004; consensus vote 27 Feb 2024): paraffin blocks at least 10 years (retrievable within 48 hours; neoplastic blocks longer, ideally to a biobank); slides at least 10 years (may be shortened if equivalent digital system); general cytology at least 10 years; cervicovaginal cytology negative at least 3 years, pathological at least 10 years; liquid cytology and fluids until diagnosis complete including molecular tests; fixed tissue at least 4 weeks; autopsy formalin tissue 3 months, blocks at least 10 years, slides 10 years; reports no fixed minimum (literature mostly 10 years). Source: https://seap.es/Noticias/2024/Tiempos_almacenamiento_PATOLOGIA.pdf . An older, undated SEAP recommendation set advises 10 years from reception for blocks and slides, blocks older than 10 years retrievable within one week, and unembedded fixed tissue at least 30 days: https://seap.es/documents/10157/1749018/Recomendaciones+-+Almacenamiento/Recomendaciones%20-%20Almacenamiento.pdf

## 6. Reporting conventions, spellcheck and word lists

### 6.1 SEAP reporting conventions

- SEAP good-practice document (ADASP-based, applies to surgical pathology and by extension cytology/FNA): report header carries AP case number on every page; clinical data summary; gross description for each specimen with coded organ (SNOMED), unique block IDs, margins inked or not; microscopic description only when the responsible pathologist deems appropriate; antibody lists and grading/staging systems; separate intraoperative consult with entry and exit times carried verbatim into the final report; final diagnosis per specimen with organ, site and procedure; amended reports flagged; request, receipt, gross, report and transcription dates on every report; minimum data set with signing pathologist, grossing pathologist, resident and audit trail. Structured reporting: pre-formatted templates linked to SNOMED CT; predefined gross protocols by specimen type. Protocols: use SEAP working-group protocols first, then CAP or ADASP; target no more than 5% of reports not conforming to protocol. Source: https://seap.es/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Reglas-y-Consejos-Buenas-Practicas-Profesionales.pdf (text dates from the 2011 White Paper era; the page is hosted in a 2026 upload).
- SEAP and ICCR: a SEAP notice asked members to comment on ICCR draft datasets (lung, melanoma, prostate, endometrium) by 31 March 2013; it does not mention Spanish adoption. Source: https://www.seap.es/actualidad/noticias/526-iccr-guidelines-for-the-development-of-cancer-datasets
